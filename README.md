# Siyan (Diego) Chen
**University of California, Santa Barbara** · Applied Mathematics · Statistics & Data Science · Economics

I study how statistical evidence changes investment decisions. My interests are machine learning for finance, empirical research design, portfolio optimization, and reproducible quantitative research.

## Selected research

| Project | Research question | What to read |
|---|---|---|
| **[StableAlpha](https://github.com/SiyanChen929/stablealpha)** | When do improvements in LLM-guided factor search reach forecast quality and net returns? | Controlled experiments, working paper, factor-language and validation code |
| **[MarketIntel](https://github.com/SiyanChen929/marketintel)** | Does a plausible equity score survive dated-data tests, and how should signals become holdings? | Point-in-time factor research, portfolio construction, risk and event governance |
| **[Pairs trading](https://github.com/SiyanChen929/marketdata-quant-suite/tree/main/projects/equity-pairs-research)** | Does statistical dependence survive selection, borrow, and trading costs? | Multiple-testing controls, nested evaluation, and documented negative results |
| **[MSCI rebalance research](https://github.com/SiyanChen929/marketdata-quant-suite/blob/main/docs/msci-rebalance-research.md)** | Is an index-flow pattern robust to event-window selection and executable prices? | Research case study and a provider-neutral event-study implementation |

The common thread is to connect **hypothesis → dated data → statistical test → portfolio decision**. I preserve failed hypotheses and distinguish exploratory findings, software verification, and investment validation.

## Background and contribution

My earlier commodity-research experience at Guangfa Futures and Jiayue Commodity Group involved supply-demand data, trade flows, inventories, and repeatable analytical workflows. Later mentored quantitative research connected those economic questions to model evaluation and portfolio design.

I direct research questions, specifications, result review, and project decisions. Substantial software implementation and manuscript preparation use AI coding assistants; each project documents its provenance and attribution. Public examples use synthetic fixtures, with private credentials and vendor data kept outside the repositories.

**Tools:** Python, R, SQL, SAS, Excel/VBA. **Methods:** statistical inference, time series, optimization, chronological evaluation, and reproducibility.
